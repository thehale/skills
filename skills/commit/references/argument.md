# A body with more than one part

For a body carrying several causes, several parallel cases, or prior commits to
cite. The rules in `SKILL.md` still govern every sentence of it.

None of this is a quota. A cause with nothing to say about it gets no paragraph,
and a message that fits in one paragraph stays one paragraph.

These run in order of reach, under the rules in `SKILL.md`.

1. **Build the body as an argument.** Open on the mechanism rather than the
   verdict, so the reader starts from a fact they can check and the evidence
   under it has something to do. Order the body as problem, then sources, then
   solution, then anything above and beyond. Alternating a problem with its fix
   and then the next problem reads as back and forth, so group the causes and
   resolve them together. Open each new idea with a transition word.
2. **Use a list only where each item renders a verdict.** Several parallel cases
   scan as a single comparison when the verdict lives in the item, which makes
   the list itself the argument. Items that describe the cases while the verdict
   waits in the prose below are worse than no list.
3. **Put a citation where its claim is.** Cite a commit in parentheses at the
   claim it supports, the way a paper cites a source, unless the commit is the
   actor of its own clause and reads inline. Archaeology gathered into a
   paragraph of its own makes the reader carry it back.
